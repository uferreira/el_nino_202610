How to read this page

Each point of the loop is one moment of the record: its horizontal position is the filtered value, its vertical position how fast that value is changing. A warm event therefore runs clockwise: above the zero line moving right while it builds, across the zero line at its peak, then below the zero line moving left as it decays.

The record is low-pass filtered (Fourier, full periods shorter than about 18 to 20 months removed) so the loop shows interannual ENSO variability, not month-to-month noise. NINO1+2 and sea level keep their seasonal cycle, so they also trace a small loop every year; the bold line in the time panel is the same filtered record with the seasonal cycle taken out, so El Niño events stand out.

The filter ends exactly on the latest observation, and the last months of the filtered record move as new months arrive. That stretch is dashed on the loop and shaded in the time panel (METHODS.md in the repository explains how long it is and why).

Drag across the time series to highlight a period; double-click or Clear removes it. Hover either view to find the same month in the other.

Data: NOAA CPC SST indices (monthly), UHSLC tide gauges (hourly, averaged to months). Downloaded 2026-11-03.
